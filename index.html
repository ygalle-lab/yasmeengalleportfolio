Helping IPV support staff respond to AI image abuse

Role
Research Intern
Where
Carnegie Mellon University, School of Computer Science
Timeline
May 2025 to present
Methods
Semi-structured interviews, survey, thematic coding, co-design
Tools
MaxQDA, Qualtrics, Excel, Figma

The problem

Intimate-partner violence (IPV) now includes technology-facilitated abuse, including AI-manipulated images. Shelter and support staff are trained on many forms of abuse, but often not on this one, and survivors can be left without a way to confirm or stop it.

What I did

  • Conducted 15 semi-structured interviews with IPV support stakeholders and coded them in MaxQDA to find themes in user needs, support gaps, and forms of abuse.
  • Analyzed a survey of 55 respondents in Qualtrics and Excel to test the interview themes at a larger scale.
  • Am designing a co-design study, including research questions, recruitment criteria, and protocols with privacy and safety built in. Staff take part as collaborators, and their feedback shapes each Figma prototype iteration. This study is in early development.

What we found

Interviews and survey data together showed consistent gaps in staff knowledge, training, and resources for technology-facilitated abuse (TFA). IPV support staff have an educational gap about TFA, and that gap limits how well they can help survivors. Staff need training, resources, and guides.

Result

Presented findings as research posters at the CRA Undergraduate to PhD Conference and the CMU REU Poster Session. These findings point to training, resources, and guides as the next step for IPV support staff. The AI image manipulation co-design study is now taking shape.

Testing an app that helps young adults adopt cybersecurity habits

Role
Research assistant
Where
Carnegie Mellon University, School of Computer Science
Timeline
May 2026 to present
Methods
Interviews, survey, comparison of two study conditions

The problem

Cybersecurity habits are hard to build. This study tests an app with a 21-day challenge that helps young adults make cybersecurity part of their routine, and encourages them to see these practices as part of being an adult.

What I did

  • Conducted interviews and took detailed notes on how participants used the app.
  • Documented participant feedback on usability, features, and ease of use.
  • Cleaned and analyzed interview transcripts and survey data to find barriers to adoption.
  • Support a comparison of two groups: participants who do only the cybersecurity challenge, and participants who do cybersecurity plus other chores and adulting tasks.

Where it stands

This study is a work in progress, with interviews and a survey still underway.
