Helping IPV support staff respond to AI image abuse

A mixed-methods study of where intimate-partner violence (IPV) support staff lack the training and resources to respond to technology-facilitated abuse (TFA), and what they need.

Background

Intimate-partner violence now includes technology-facilitated abuse, including AI-manipulated images. Support staff are trained on many forms of abuse, but often not on this one, so survivors can be left without a way to confirm or stop it.

Research questions

  • Where do IPV support staff lack the knowledge and resources to respond to technology-facilitated abuse?
  • What do staff need to help survivors more effectively?
  • [Add any other research questions that guided the work.]

Methods

  • 15 semi-structured interviews with IPV support stakeholders, to understand their needs, gaps, and the forms of abuse they see.
  • A survey of 55 respondents, to test whether the interview themes held more broadly.
  • [Add how participants were recruited, how long interviews lasted, and the roles represented, without identifying anyone.]

Analysis

I coded the interview transcripts in MaxQDA to identify themes in user needs, support gaps, and forms of abuse. I analyzed the survey in Qualtrics and Excel, then compared the survey results with the interview themes.

[Describe your coding approach, such as how you built the codebook, how many themes emerged, and how you checked them.]

Findings

Both methods showed consistent gaps in staff knowledge, training, and resources around technology-facilitated abuse. IPV support staff have an educational gap about TFA, and that gap limits how well they can help survivors. Staff need training, resources, and guides.

[Add 2-3 specific themes you can share, each with one sentence of explanation.]

What happens next

The findings point to training, resources, and guides as the next step for IPV support staff. I am designing a co-design study in which staff take part as collaborators and their feedback shapes each Figma prototype iteration. I am defining the research questions, recruitment criteria, and protocols, with privacy and safety built in. This study is in early development.

I presented the findings as research posters at the CRA Undergraduate to PhD Conference and the CMU REU Poster Session.
